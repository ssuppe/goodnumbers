**1. Detailed Analysis of Weekly Data (2024-11-28 to 2024-12-05):**

* **Elevated Average Glucose (145 mg/dl) and High Standard Deviation (52.5 mg/dl):**  These two metrics combined paint a picture of inconsistent glucose control.  While the reported Time in Range (TIR) of 74% might seem reasonable at first glance, the large standard deviation suggests wide swings between hyperglycemia (24% >180 mg/dl) and occasional hypoglycemia (2% <70 mg/dl). This variability is likely the primary driver of the elevated average glucose.  We need to understand *why* these swings are occurring.

* **Potential Causes of Variability:**
    * **Inaccurate Carb Counting:**  A common culprit in glucose variability.  Overestimation leads to hypoglycemia, underestimation to hyperglycemia.  Detailed dietary review and education on accurate carb estimation are crucial.
    * **Incorrect Insulin-to-Carb Ratio (ICR):** If the ICR is too low, postprandial hyperglycemia will result.  If too high, postprandial hypoglycemia is likely.  Review and adjustment of ICR based on time of day and meal composition is warranted. Consider a temporary increase in mealtime boluses to ensure appropriate coverage if other aspects of the mealtime dosage and timing are stable.  Monitor the outcome.  Consider having the patient test their ICR by consuming a standardized meal to ensure it's being covered appropriately.
    * **Delayed or Inconsistent Meal Bolusing:**  Bolusing too late or inconsistently relative to meal consumption can cause significant postprandial hyperglycemia.  Reinforce the importance of pre-meal bolusing (10-15 minutes prior, depending on insulin type). Consider using an alarm if the patient frequently forgets to bolus.
    * **Exercise and Physical Activity:**  Exercise can significantly lower glucose levels.  The patient needs education on adjusting insulin doses and/or carbohydrate intake around exercise.  The type, intensity, and duration of exercise all influence glucose response.  A detailed exercise log correlated with CGM data can help identify patterns and guide adjustments.
    * **Stress and Illness:** Both can elevate glucose levels.  Explore stress management techniques (mindfulness, meditation, yoga) and ensure the patient has a sick-day plan.
    * **Hormonal Fluctuations:**  Hormonal changes, particularly in males related to testosterone levels (or use of exogenous testosterone), can impact insulin sensitivity.  Investigate any potential hormonal imbalances.
    * **Gastroparesis:** While less common, delayed gastric emptying can lead to unpredictable glucose fluctuations.  If other factors are ruled out, consider investigating this possibility.
