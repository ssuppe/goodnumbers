* **Education/Context:** Dawn phenomenon is a natural rise in blood glucose in the early morning hours due to hormonal changes (Cryer, 2004). It is a common challenge for people with diabetes, especially type 1.
* **Evaluation:** The analysis suggests a possible dawn phenomenon on 3 out of 7 days, but more data is needed to confirm. The noted rises starting between 6:00-6:59 am align with the typical timeframe for dawn phenomenon. The magnitude of the rises (1.3-3.3 mmol/L) is also within the expected range.  The fact that one rise followed a low blood glucose event correctly identifies a rebound effect rather than true dawn phenomenon.  The listed factors contributing to nighttime lows are accurate and helpful.
* **Recommendations:**
    1. **Basal Rate Adjustment:** Carefully evaluate basal insulin rates between the hours of 4:00 am and 8:00 am. A slight increase in basal insulin during this period might be necessary. Close monitoring is essential to avoid nocturnal hypoglycemia.  Consider using a temporary basal rate increase initially to test the effectiveness.
    2. **Continue Monitoring:** Gather more data over several weeks to confirm the presence and consistency of dawn phenomenon. This will help determine the optimal basal rate adjustments. A CGM is invaluable for this.
    3. **Lifestyle Factors:** Ensure adequate sleep, manage stress levels, and evaluate the impact of evening meals, especially those high in fat.  Late-night snacks or adjustments to evening boluses may be necessary depending on the cause of nighttime lows.
* **Treatments:** No specific treatments beyond basal insulin adjustments are indicated at this time.

**c. Nighttime Lows:**

* **Education/Context:** Nocturnal hypoglycemia is a serious concern in type 1 diabetes, as it can lead to seizures, coma, and even death (Diabetes UK, 2022).  The listed contributing factors are all well-established.
* **Evaluation:** While the patient's overall time spent low is excellent (3%), the occurrence of nighttime lows requiring correction warrants attention.  Identifying the specific cause is critical.
* **Recommendations:**

    2. **Carb Counting and Bolus Timing:** Review carb counting accuracy and pre-bolus timing for evening meals.  High-fat meals may require a split bolus or extended bolus to account for delayed absorption.
